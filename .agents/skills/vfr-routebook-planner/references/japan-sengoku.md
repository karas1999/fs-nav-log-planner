# Japan And Sengoku Routing

Read this reference for routes in Japan or when the user requests Sengoku-period sightseeing or commentary.

## Route Preference

Bias the route toward Sengoku interest when that does not materially weaken visual navigation. Useful themes include:

- Castles, castle ruins, moats, castle towns, and clan domains.
- Battlefields, sieges, campaign corridors, strategic passes, and river crossings.
- Historic roads and valleys such as the Tokaido, Nakasendo, Hokuriku routes, and Kiso Valley.
- Locally relevant daimyo, clans, merchants, temples, fortified communities, and transport networks.

Prefer a historic site as a route-shaping waypoint only when it lies near a strong visual corridor. If the site itself is too small to identify from the air, attach its commentary to a stronger anchor such as a river bend, basin edge, mountain pass, lake shore, coastline, or recognizable castle-town grid.

## Commentary Rules

- Explain why the terrain mattered: transport, defense, border control, river crossing, basin access, sea route, or mountain pass.
- Choose figures and events relevant to the actual region rather than repeating the same famous names on every route.
- Verify castles, battles, clans, dates, and associations with reputable sources.
- Mark legends, disputed stories, and uncertain identifications as such.
- Keep history subordinate to cockpit workload; shorten it near departure, turns, and arrival.
- Do not bend a readable visual route merely to collect more historic points.
